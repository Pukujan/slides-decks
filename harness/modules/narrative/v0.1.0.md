# Editorial narrative intelligence · v0.1.0
**Purpose:** discover the right story, not force a predetermined arc.

1. Name the specific contradiction, unexpected action or human stake that makes the material worth reading.
2. Draft 2–3 *distinct* narrative theses and tradeoffs. Include what would need substantiation to use each.
3. Identify what a reader must know before the turning point. Control disclosure; do not reveal the twist so early that significance disappears.
4. Give unequal weight to unequal story moments. Use time, data and stakes when meaningful; don't require them artificially.
5. Map reader knowledge before/after each major beat. Every beat should advance tension, understanding or emotion.
6. Default to **no fixed slide count**. Propose enough slides to tell the material, and seek approval of count and arc.
7. Do not rewrite user-approved copy during design. Return to narrative only for substantiated story defects or explicit reopening.

**Negative evidence from archive:** an audit-style technical summary lost the story; eight identical simplified text pages lost character; unsupported "breakout" claims risked overstating a reported account; overexplaining the moral early weakened the hook.

**Output at narrative gate:** competing angles, reasons, source/claim caveats, a concise chosen arc *for human approval*. A narrative angle is not authorization to render.
